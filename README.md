# Global-Disaster-Patterns
This study examined the spatial and temporal distribution of six major disaster types recorded in the GDACS database from 2000 to 2025. The analysis identified substantial geographic concentration in disaster occurrence, with Indonesia, Japan, the United States, Papua New Guinea, Chile, and Greece among the countries and territories reporting the highest numbers of recorded events. Disaster occurrence also varied considerably over time, with annual events increasing sharply after 2002, remaining relatively high through much of the 2000s and 2010s, and declining in the most recent years.

The composition of disaster events changed markedly across the study period. Earthquakes constituted the dominant share of recorded events during much of the 2003 to 2018 period, while forest fires became increasingly prominent after 2019 and accounted for a substantial proportion of events in the early 2020s. These spatial and temporal patterns demonstrate that disaster burden is not uniformly distributed and that different geographic areas experience distinct and evolving hazard profiles, highlighting the importance of context specific preparedness, risk assessment, and coordinated humanitarian response planning.
